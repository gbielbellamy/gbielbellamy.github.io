Featured project

Northstar

A single workspace for running a career transition: plan the day, ship the project, track the search, and see whether any of it is working. Nine sections, one domain model, and — for now — no backend at all.

  • Elastic scheduling. Skip a work session and its content slides to that area's next slot. The plan stretches rather than quietly falling apart.
  • Logic separated from React. Scheduling, funnel maths and date handling live in pure functions with no store access, so they can be reasoned about — and tested — on their own.
  • Versioned local state. A new seed plan can ship without overwriting a single thing the user recorded themselves.
  • A design system with a live catalogue. Tokens, dark mode and switchable typefaces, all driven from CSS custom properties.
  • In progress. Today it runs locally, with state in the browser. Next up is a PostgreSQL database behind it, plus accounts, login and the security work that has to come with them.

About

The short version

I came to engineering the long way round. From 2005 I worked in customer relations at Atento, looking after a portfolio of corporate accounts — the person a business called when its mobile and internet services stopped behaving. Then, from 2017 to 2023, I was a Quality Assurance Analyst at ISGF, monitoring interaction quality across six teams and around seventy-two agents.

That second job is the one that shaped how I work. Auditing quality means reading a process closely enough to find where it actually breaks, and then proving the fix held. I designed new follow-up procedures that cut repeat customer contacts, built the documentation standards that kept assessments consistent across teams, and coached agents and managers on what the numbers were really telling them.

None of that was writing code. But finding where a system breaks, writing it down so the next person can follow it, and checking whether the fix survived contact with reality turn out to be the same habits in a different room — and they transferred more cleanly than I expected.

I studied Digital Interaction and Multimedia at the Universitat Oberta de Catalunya and completed the Codecademy Full-Stack Engineer programme. Now I build web applications end to end, and I'm most interested in the ones with real state behind them — scheduling, tracking, anything where the data model has to hold up over time.

I'm based in Berkeley, California and authorized to work in the U.S. What I'm looking for is a team: full-time, part-time, an internship, an apprenticeship or volunteer work — I care far more about being somewhere I can practise, learn and contribute than about the label on the role.

I work natively in Spanish, Catalan and French, and professionally in English.
